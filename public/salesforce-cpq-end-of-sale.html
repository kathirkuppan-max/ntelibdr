Salesforce CPQ end of sale: when and how to migrate to Revenue Cloud

By Kathiravan Kuppan, Salesforce Premier Partner · Updated November 16, 2026
The short answer: Salesforce CPQ is end of sale, not end of life. Existing customers can renew, add users and keep support, and no end-of-life date has been announced. But new development goes into Revenue Cloud, and moving to it is a reimplementation, not an upgrade: your product rules, price rules and templates are rebuilt, not copied. Migrate when you have a business reason, plan either way, and don't carry today's pricing errors into the new system.

What CPQ end of sale means for existing customers

Salesforce's own page, "Product End of Sale (Not End of Life)", sets out the terms:

  • No new CPQ customers. Salesforce no longer sells CPQ to companies that don't already have it.
  • Existing customers keep going. You can renew, add users and keep support. Nobody is forced to move.
  • No retirement date. As of November 2026, Salesforce has announced no end-of-life date. Third-party dates you may see online are guesses.
  • New development goes to Revenue Cloud. That's where new features, AI and platform investment land.

So is Salesforce CPQ end of life? Not today. The practical change is that every CPQ renewal now carries a question: when do we move, and what will it take?

A note on names. Salesforce has called the successor Revenue Lifecycle Management, Revenue Cloud Advanced and Agentforce Revenue Management. Its current pages mostly say Revenue Cloud, sold in Growth and Advanced editions. It's the same product; this guide says Revenue Cloud.

What actually changed

Salesforce CPQ was built as a managed package sitting on top of the Salesforce platform. Revenue Cloud is built into the core platform. Salesforce's migration page puts the difference this way: CPQ's business logic lived inside its UI components, while Revenue Cloud exposes 150+ APIs and invocable actions on standard core objects, so quoting, pricing and ordering can be automated and called from anywhere, not just the Salesforce quote screen.

Salesforce also positions Revenue Cloud for things CPQ was never designed for: selling through channels and outside Salesforce, hybrid and consumption-based pricing, and automation with AI agents. That's where its roadmap is going.

Migrate now, or wait?

Migrate now if…

  • You sell usage, consumption or hybrid models that CPQ handles awkwardly.
  • Partners, dealers or customers need to quote outside the Salesforce UI.
  • Your CPQ is so customized that every change is a project.
  • Reps still quote from Excel builders because CPQ can't hold the rules.
  • You're already planning a major quote-to-cash change (new ERP, new billing, an acquisition to integrate).

Wait and plan if…

  • CPQ works for how you sell today.
  • Your catalog and pricing are stable.
  • There's no near-term driver beyond the announcement.
  • You can't yet staff a reimplementation properly. A rushed migration is the expensive kind.

Waiting isn't doing nothing. Use the time to document your pricing logic, clean the catalog, and find out what your current setup is getting wrong. A readout of your CPQ setup (below) gives you the facts for the next renewal either way.

CPQ to Revenue Cloud migration: what your setup becomes

There's no one-click converter. Each part of a CPQ setup has a home in Revenue Cloud, but the logic is rebuilt in Revenue Cloud's own model. This is the map we work from:

How long a CPQ migration takes, and what drives the effort

Because the rules are rebuilt rather than moved, the honest estimate depends on how much logic you've built up. A clean catalog with standard pricing can have quotes live in weeks. Years of accumulated rules and custom code take quarters, phased by product line or segment. What moves the number:

  • Rule count and age. How many product rules, price rules and discount schedules, and how many still fire.
  • Custom code. Quote Calculator Plugin scripts, Apex and custom actions.
  • Logic outside CPQ. Excel builders, engineering sign-offs and dealer price sheets reps quote from.
  • Business in flight. Open quotes, active contracts and renewals that must keep working.
  • The ERP handoff. SAP, Epicor, Infor, NetSuite or Dynamics, and whether orders reconcile back to quotes today.
  • Channel pricing. Dealer and distributor pricing, rebates and special pricing agreements.
  • Who maintains it afterwards. The design should match the team that will own the rules.

The step most migrations skip

A migration is the one time you rebuild every price rule, discount schedule and approval from scratch. Most projects use that moment to port today's logic as faithfully as possible, including whatever it's been getting wrong.

Before we design anything in Revenue Cloud, we run a Leak Ledger on the current setup: join quotes, orders, invoices and claims on one deal ID and itemize what's slipping. Discounts running deeper than peer deals. Orders entered at a different price than the accepted quote. Invoices billed off the contract. Required options left off quotes. Each finding becomes a design requirement for the new system instead of a habit you migrate.

Don't migrate your leaks. The rebuild is the cheapest moment you'll ever have to fix them.

In our published illustrative model, configuration and discounting errors come to about 3.4% of quoted value, and MGI Research frames fixing revenue leakage as worth 2–4% of revenue. Your own data tells you the real number. The argument behind this approach is in A ledger, not a dashboard.

A CPQ migration plan that holds up

  1. Read the current setup. Every product, bundle, product rule, price rule, discount schedule, template and Excel builder, with counts and an owner who knows why each exists.
  2. Run the ledger. Find what the current setup is leaking, so the new design fixes it instead of copying it.
  3. Clean the catalog. Retire dead products, bundles and rules before they're rebuilt.
  4. Design in Revenue Cloud's model, not CPQ's. Rebuild pricing and configuration the way the platform expects; don't recreate the package in custom code.
  5. Connect the ERP and billing so orders and invoices reconcile to the quote. Use a read-mostly connection where possible.
  6. Run both in parallel on your own recent quotes, compare outputs line by line, then cut over by segment. CPQ stays live until Revenue Cloud has proven itself.

Salesforce CPQ end of sale: FAQ

Is Salesforce CPQ end of life?

No. It's end of sale, not end of life. Salesforce no longer sells it to new customers, but existing customers can renew, add users and keep support, and as of November 2026 no end-of-life or retirement date has been announced.

Can we still renew CPQ and add licenses?

Yes. Salesforce states existing customers can renew, add users and keep support. Moving isn't forced; new features and investment simply go to Revenue Cloud.

Is moving from CPQ to Revenue Cloud an upgrade?

No, it's a reimplementation. Revenue Cloud is built into the core platform with its own catalog, pricing and configuration model, so product rules, price rules and custom scripts are rebuilt, not copied.

What do CPQ product rules and price rules become?

Product rules become constraint rules in the Revenue Cloud configurator. Price rules, discount schedules and Quote Calculator Plugin scripts become declarative pricing procedures, often with decision tables. See the full map.

How long does a CPQ to Revenue Cloud migration take?

Weeks to quotes live for a clean catalog with standard pricing; quarters, phased, for heavy custom logic, Excel builders, renewals in flight and an ERP integration. See what drives the effort.

Do we have to stop quoting during the migration?

No. Keep CPQ live while Revenue Cloud is built and tested on your own recent quotes, then cut over by product line or segment.

What's the most common migration mistake?

Porting today's pricing logic as is, including the errors. The rebuild is the moment to find what's leaking and fix it in the design.
