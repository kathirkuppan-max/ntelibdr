Salesforce SAP integration for manufacturers

By Kathiravan Kuppan, Salesforce Premier Partner · Updated November 16, 2026
The short answer: A Salesforce SAP integration for a manufacturer should do one job well: turn an accepted Salesforce quote into a correct SAP sales order with nothing retyped, and bring order status, deliveries and invoices back to the rep. SAP stays the system of record for materials, pricing conditions and credit. The usual routes are MuleSoft, which Salesforce owns and which has SAP connectors, or SAP Integration Suite, calling S/4HANA OData APIs or ECC BAPIs and IDocs.

Start with the quote-to-order flow. That's where manufacturers lose the most: orders retyped into the ERP at a different price, the wrong ship-to, or options left off. Everything else can follow.

Ways to connect Salesforce and SAP

MuleSoft

Salesforce-owned, with SAP connectors for S/4HANA and ECC. The usual choice when Salesforce is the hub and other systems join later.

SAP Integration Suite

SAP's own integration platform (BTP). A good fit when the SAP team runs integrations and wants them in SAP's tooling.

Direct APIs

S/4HANA OData APIs, or BAPIs and IDocs on ECC. Fine for a single flow; harder to monitor and change as flows grow.

We're tool-agnostic. The right choice depends on what your IT team already runs and how many systems will join later. MuleSoft is the common choice when Salesforce is the hub.

Where Salesforce SAP integrations go wrong

  • Partner functions. One Salesforce account rarely equals one SAP customer. Map sold-to, ship-to, bill-to and payer explicitly, usually with an account hierarchy or address records in Salesforce.
  • Who prices. If SAP condition records stay the price source, Salesforce must either call SAP for pricing or replicate the conditions. Doing both is how quotes and orders drift apart.
  • Sales area. Sales org, distribution channel and division change what a customer can buy and the price they get. Carry them on the account and the quote.
  • Configurable materials. If you use SAP variant configuration, the characteristic values on the order must match what the Salesforce quote configured, or production builds something different from what was sold.
  • Credit blocks. Show SAP credit status on the account before quoting, not after the order fails.

Quote to order: the flow that pays for the integration

For a manufacturer, the integration isn't about syncing records; it's about the quote the customer accepted becoming the order you build and bill. With Revenue Cloud or CPQ in Salesforce, the accepted quote creates the SAP order with the same configuration and price, and the order and invoice come back linked to it. That's what lets you check, deal by deal, that what was quoted is what was ordered and billed. We call that check a Leak Ledger.

FAQ

Does Salesforce have a native SAP integration?

Not out of the box in Sales Cloud. Salesforce owns MuleSoft, which has prebuilt SAP connectors, and SAP offers its own Integration Suite. Most manufacturers use one of the two rather than point-to-point code.

Should quotes live in Salesforce or SAP?

Quotes should live in Salesforce, where reps configure and price, and become SAP sales orders on acceptance. Keeping a second quote in SAP doubles the work and splits the history.

Which system should own pricing?

Pick one. Either SAP condition records stay the source and Salesforce calls SAP at quote time, or pricing moves to Revenue Cloud and SAP receives the final price on the order. Running both is the most common source of quote-to-order price mismatches.

How long does a Salesforce SAP integration take?

Core flows (customers, materials, quote to order, status back) can go live in weeks. Variant configuration, multiple sales areas and pricing ownership add time, so they're scoped up front.
