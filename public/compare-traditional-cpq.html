Nteli vs. traditional CPQ.

The short answer — from a company that implements CPQ: Nteli is a Salesforce Premier Partner; we've delivered CPQ and Revenue Cloud for enterprises for years. Traditional CPQ is the right tool when sellers quote inside a CRM motion and you can staff the program. We built the Agent Pricing Engine for the manufacturers that regime doesn't fit — where the quote starts as a messy RFQ on an estimator's desk, not a seller's configurator screen.

Two different regimes, honestly stated

Traditional CPQ — Salesforce CPQ/Revenue Cloud, and in manufacturing, platforms like Tacton or Configure One — assumes a particular shape of company: sellers configure inside the pipeline, pricing and approvals route through sales ops, and someone owns rule authoring and administration as a job. When that's you, it works, and the payoff is a quoting motion embedded in the system your revenue already runs through. That's not a concession — it's half of our business. We implement it.

The Agent Pricing Engine assumes a different shape: a $20M–$500M manufacturer where quotes start as an emailed RFQ, the estimator is the bottleneck, and nobody is going to staff a rule-authoring team. The intake is inverted — paste the RFQ, the spec, or the email, and the AI runs the conversation while a deterministic engine sizes, checks and prices from cited rules: every number and every refusal shows its source, the code section or the catalog page. It's delivered as an MCP server, so it runs inside Claude rather than inside your CRM.

Two honest caveats the table can't hold: first, traditional CPQ platforms are mature in ways a young product isn't — breadth of billing integrations, ecosystem, marketplace apps. Second, if your sellers — not estimators — are the ones quoting, the CRM-embedded motion is a feature, not a limitation.

The question isn't which tool is better. It's where your quote actually starts: a seller's pipeline, or an estimator's inbox.

Using both

Some companies should run both: the engine quotes engineered products at the estimating desk, and the won order flows into the CRM/ERP quote-to-cash motion — contracts, billing, revenue recognition — which is exactly the integration work our practice does. They're two halves of the same Quote-to-Comply pipeline, and we're unusual in building for both.
