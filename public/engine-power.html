Power packages — NEC 700, 701 and 702 loads on separate transfer switches, every capacity checked after redundancy before pricing.

What it quotes

Switchgear, automatic transfer switches (UL 1008), UPS frames, modules and batteries, diesel generators with fuel storage, step-down transformers and battery storage cabinets — at 480 V or 4,160 V service, under N, N+1 or 2N.

What it checks

Load classes kept apart: emergency (NEC 700), legally required (NEC 701), optional (NEC 702) and critical UPS loads each get their own transfer switch, sized to that class. UPS, generator, transformer and switchgear capacity are checked after redundancy, with losses included.

On every quote

Each capacity used against provided — generator kW for the backed loads, UPS kW for the critical load, amps on each transfer switch — plus the site facts the package depends on (service voltage, available fault current), stated rather than buried.

Cited rules decide what may be built and how it must be split — and every size, price and refusal shows its source.

NEC revisions

Articles 700, 701 and 702 decide which loads share a transfer switch and which never may — and they move on the code cycle.

NFPA 110

Emergency power supply systems carry their own levels, run times and testing — a hospital wing is not a warehouse.

NFPA 855

Battery storage brings its own siting, spacing and fire-protection rules — indoors and outdoors are different quotes.

Long lead times

Generators, switchgear and transformers ship on long lead times, so a quote priced on last year’s rules is delivered under next year’s.

THE REFUSAL
“Quote the emergency power for a building: 50 kW of egress lighting and fire alarm at 208 V, 480 V service, 65 kA, no generator.”

What you’ll see: The engine won’t call it ready: generator capacity for the backed loads is 60 kW needed, 0 kW available. The fix is priced next to it — add one generator, about $177,000 — and it stays the customer’s call.

“A power package for a hospital wing, N+1, 480 V service, 65 kA: 200 kW critical imaging and IT at 480 V, 50 kW emergency egress lighting and fire alarm at 208 V, 150 kW optional HVAC at 480 V.”

What you’ll see: Five 50 kW UPS modules, two 500 kW generators, a 300 kVA step-down transformer, 800 A / 65 kA switchgear and a transfer switch per load class (emergency 100 A, critical 400 A, optional 225 A) — about $671,000 with engineering, and every capacity inside its limits after redundancy.

“Standby power for a plant: 300 kW of smoke control and sewage pumps (legally required) and a 400 kW process line (optional), 480 V, 65 kA, no redundancy, plus two battery storage cabinets.”

What you’ll see: One 1,012 kW generator for 710 kW of backed load, 1,600 A switchgear, separate 600 A transfer switches for the NEC 701 and NEC 702 loads, and two 125 kW / 261 kWh battery cabinets — about $733,000 with engineering.
