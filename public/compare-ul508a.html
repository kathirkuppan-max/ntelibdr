The one distinction that matters

Every tool on this list can price a panel. The question is when the panel gets checked. Most shops find the SCCR problem, the undersized main or the fan on a NEMA 4X door after the PO, during design or at inspection, when fixing it comes out of the margin you quoted.

A tool that checks the panel at the quote moves that discovery to the cheapest moment: before the price goes out, when the fix is a line on the quote instead of a change order.

A complete quote is not a correct one. The expensive mistakes aren’t the missing ferrules; they’re the 5 kA starters on a 22 kA service.

The demo test

Bring one real RFQ, names removed, and ask each vendor to quote it live. Then check:

  1. Fault current. Leave it out of the RFQ. Does the tool ask, or quietly assume?
  2. SCCR. Give it 22 kA available. Do 5 kA starters get fuse kits, and does the main’s interrupting rating change?
  3. Motor currents. Are full-load currents taken from NEC Table 430.250 at the actual supply voltage, and is the main sized from them?
  4. Enclosure. Ask for NEMA 4X with a hot ambient. Does it refuse a filtered fan and size the cooling?
  5. Sources. Can it show the code section or catalog page behind each size?
  6. Completeness. Engineering, FAT, the UL label, crating and freight all on the quote.

Any vendor, including us, should be able to run that on your RFQ. If your spreadsheet passes all six, you may not need any of these tools.
