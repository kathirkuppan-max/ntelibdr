The Leak Ledger Method · Assumptions

Revenue leakage assumptions, explained.

These are the ten assumptions behind the leak-ledger calculator and the ledger table in A ledger, not a dashboard. One model, one set of numbers, every line's arithmetic shown, and every assumption editable in the calculator.

What these numbers are, and aren't. They are illustrative assumptions, tuned so the model describes a fictional ~$90M configure-to-order manufacturer (“Calder”) with dealers, a distributor channel and a service business. They are not measured industry averages, and we don't present them as anyone's research. For an outside reference point, MGI Research frames fixing revenue leakage as worth 2–4% in added revenue and cash flow; this model lands at about 3% of revenue. The numbers that matter are yours: change the assumptions in the calculator, or replace them with your own data in a Leak Ledger Workshop.

Two kinds of money

Recoverable cash can still be collected: correct an invoice billed off contract, bill an order entered below its quote, claw back an overpaid rebate, dispute an unsupported claim. Preventable margin is already gone: last year's discounts and rework can't be taken back, only stopped going forward. Each error counts in one line only.

Other starting points

The calculator offers two more starting points that change only the mix, not the rates: a semiconductor or electronics company with a large distributor channel (25% quoted, 45% through distributors, 2% service) and a pharmaceutical manufacturer selling through wholesalers (no configured quotes, 70% through wholesalers filing chargebacks). Adjust both to your business.

“Can't I do this in a spreadsheet?”

You can total it in one. The ledger isn't the arithmetic on this page. It's matching quotes, orders, invoices and claims that share no ID, checking every line instead of a sample, and stopping the error before the money moves. Bring your spreadsheet to a workshop and run the same quarter side by side.

The capacity assumption: a hand-built quote for a configured product takes a senior estimator most of half a day — call it ~2 quotes per estimator per day. On the engine the conversation does the assembly and the rules do the checking, so the same team handles roughly 3× the quote volume. This is an assumption to test on your own RFQs, not a measured result.
