LLMs guess. Pricing can't.

Everyone wants an AI agent quoting their products. Nobody wants an AI agent inventing a discount. The Agent Pricing Engine splits the job: the agent handles the conversation — the constraint engine owns every number.

Agent alone

Fluent, friendly, and occasionally wrong

A raw LLM quoting from a price sheet will eventually hallucinate a configuration that doesn't exist, a discount nobody approved, or a delivery promise engineering can't meet. One wrong quote in production costs more than the automation saved.

Agent + Engine

Fluent, friendly, and provably right

The agent gathers facts and talks to people. Every configuration check, compatibility rule, and price comes from the deterministic engine — same inputs, same answer, every time. If a combination is invalid, the engine says why and shows every way to fix it.

How it's built

Three layers. Zero black boxes.

Conversation on top, constraints underneath, governance around everything.

Deterministic core

The engine owns the number

A constraint engine encodes your product model — options, compatibility, capacity, lead times, pricing waterfall. It never improvises. Identical inputs produce identical quotes, which is what your auditors, your CFO, and your dealers actually require.

Conversational front

The agent runs the conversation

Reps and customers answer plain questions, ranked by what actually narrows the quote. Hit a conflict? The agent relays every minimal way out. Ask "why not X?" and get the real constraint, not a shrug. Explore what-ifs before committing.

Governed change

Rules change in daylight

Engineers teach the engine in plain words. Every proposed rule is previewed against your real quote history — see exactly which past deals it would have changed — and a second engineer approves before it goes live. Nothing changes silently.

Plain-language rule authoring Impact preview on quote history Dual approval, full audit trail
