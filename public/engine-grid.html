DOE 2029

The April 2024 final rule raises efficiency levels for units made from April 23, 2029 — and DOE’s 2026 review of those levels is open now.

Section 232

The steel duty reaches transformers (HTS 8504), and the allowance on a quote has to be stated, not hidden in the margin.

NEC revisions

Article 450 decides where each liquid and dry-type may sit — vaults, clearances, less-flammable fluids — and it moves on the code cycle.

THE REFUSAL
“Quote a 1,500 kVA pad-mount, 12.47 kV to 480Y/277, mineral oil, installed inside the building.”

What you’ll see: Mineral oil indoors without a vault is refused under NEC 450.26 — with four ways out, each priced: natural ester from about $127,000 (the usual choice), a vault from about $120,000, silicone from about $137,000, or a dry-type unit.

THEN ASK — same chat as the prompt above
“Same unit outdoors at the 2029 DOE level. What fault current must the switchboard handle, and what if we go low impedance?”

What you’ll see: A 99.51% minimum-efficiency build (10 CFR 431.196, 2029 level) at about $128,000 — and the fault-current arithmetic in the open: about 31.4 kA at the standard 5.75% impedance, 36.1 kA if you go low impedance, so the switchboard rating is decided before the order, not after.

“2,500 kVA solar step-up at 34.5 kV with a 600 V inverter winding.”

What you’ll see: A step-up design to IEEE C57.159 — electrostatic shield, built for no-load nights and inverter harmonics — at about $182,000. And a detail estimators catch: no bayonet fuse is published at 34.5 kV, so the primary is protected upstream per NEC 450.3, stated on the quote.
