Nteli vs. spreadsheet estimating.

The short answer: if you quote a handful of RFQs a month on a product with few interacting options, keep your spreadsheet — it's free, fast and yours. The comparison only gets interesting when options constrain each other and quote volume is the bottleneck. That's the regime this page is about.

Where spreadsheets genuinely win

  • Cost and control: free, no vendor, no sign-up, fully yours.
  • Flexibility: a one-off oddball quote needs no rule change — you just type it.
  • Zero learning curve: every estimator you'll ever hire already knows Excel.
  • Right-sized for simple products: few options, stable prices, low volume — a sheet is honestly the correct tool.

If that's your shop, stop reading and keep the sheet. Seriously.

Where they break

Spreadsheets price what you type; they don't check whether what you typed can be built. The moment options interact — motor current vs. main sizing, SCCR vs. available fault current, connector vs. gauge, cooling vs. enclosure rating — the checks live in your senior estimator's head. The sheet computes a price for configurations that don't exist, and the error is discovered on the floor, at test, or in the field, where it's paid for out of margin.

The second break is capacity. A hand-built quote for a configured product takes a senior estimator roughly half a day — call it two quotes per estimator per day — so RFQs queue behind your best person, and the quotes you never answered are the invisible cost. The third is institutional: the quote and the submittal are built twice by different people, prices drift from stale tabs, and when the estimator retires, the rules retire with them.

The honest cost of switching

The real price isn't software — it's encoding the rules: what goes with what, what's forbidden, what it costs. Anyone selling you a configurator without saying that is selling you a blank rules editor and a project. Nteli's answer is vertical starter rule packs — panels, cable assemblies, HVAC, pumps, hazardous-area and more — so you start from a working pack built from decades of configuring these products, then override it with your own rules, governed and audited. Your estimator's knowledge goes into an asset the company owns instead of walking out at retirement.

The spreadsheet's real cost was never the license. It's the quote you couldn't send, the panel that bounced, and the rules that live in one person's head.
