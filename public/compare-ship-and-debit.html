The one distinction that matters

Every tool on this list can calculate a claim. The question is whether it can prove the resale qualifies: that the part was bought and resold under the agreement it cites, to the end customer it names, in the quantity claimed, from inventory the distributor actually held.

That proof needs records from three places that rarely share a system: the agreement (design registration or SPA, usually in the CRM), the transactions (orders, invoices and credit memos, in the ERP) and the resale itself (the distributor's EDI 867 or POS file). A tool that only sees one or two of them is checking arithmetic, not entitlement.

A claim isn't wrong because it's big. It's unverified because checking it by hand costs more than paying it.

The demo test

Bring your own anonymised data — one real distributor claim file, its EDI 867 resale file and the special pricing agreement behind it — and ask each vendor to:

  1. Match each claim line to its resale record by distributor, invoice, line, part, end customer and quantity.
  2. Map the distributor's part number and end customer to your master data, and show what happens when it can't.
  3. Check the agreement: valid dates, eligible quantity, approved debit amount.
  4. Catch the bad lines: duplicates, resale with no matching agreement, and claims exceeding the distributor's inventory.
  5. Reject single lines without losing or rewriting the original submission.
  6. Trace an approved line all the way to the credit memo and the GL.

Any vendor, including us, should be able to run that on your file. The ones that ask to use their own sample data instead are telling you something.

How much is at stake

Nteli's engagement-derived planning rates — not survey statistics — put channel claim leakage at about 5% of channel revenue, with roughly 40% of it recoverable once every claim line is validated against its agreement. The arithmetic is on our benchmarks page, and the argument for treating this as reconciliation rather than reporting is in A ledger, not a dashboard.

Where Nteli is the wrong choice

  • You want one platform for pricing, quoting and rebates. Vendavo or Pricefx will serve you better than a standalone module.
  • You run SAP and want everything settled inside it. Vistex is built for exactly that.
  • Your claim volume is small and your agreements already live in the ERP. Start with the ERP's native processing.
