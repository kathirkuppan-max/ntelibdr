02 · Quote to cash, end to end

Revenue leaks at four points. I've architected all four.

Quote-to-cash is my expertise: configure-price-quote, contract management, subscription management and revenue management. They're the four points where revenue leaks, and the four questions the Leak Ledger Method asks of every dollar.

QUOTE · CPQ

Quote

What price was promised?

Product rules, price books and discount discipline. The leak: configurations that can't be built, discounts off policy, prices from a stale book.

ORDER · CONTRACT MANAGEMENT

Order

What actually transacted?

Contracts, sales agreements and amendments. The leak: orders that drift from the quote, price commitments nobody enforces.

RECOGNIZE · SUBSCRIPTION & REVENUE MGMT

Recognize

What was settled and recognized?

Billing, renewals, uplifts, claims and ASC 606. The leak: missed uplifts and renewals, invoices that don't match terms, claims paid without checking.

COMPLY · REVENUE MANAGEMENT

Comply

What was filed?

Tax, government pricing and the audit trail. The leak: filings that don't agree with the deals behind them.

At Cisco, Dell and IBM, the whole machine existed to keep these four answers in agreement. Nteli finds where they drift apart for everyone else, and closes the gap lever by lever.

03 · What those years taught me

Six principles I brought to Nteli.

These came from watching quote-to-cash succeed and fail at enterprise scale. Every Nteli engagement, and every line of the engine, is built on them.

PRINCIPLE 1

The rules are the product

A configurator is only as good as its rules: what goes with what, what's required, what's forbidden, what it costs. Get them right and any interface works. Get them wrong and no interface saves you.

PRINCIPLE 2

A quote is a promise

The factory has to build it, the contract has to honor it, finance has to recognize it and the auditor has to accept it. A quote that can't be built or booked isn't fast. It's a future write-off.

PRINCIPLE 3

Recover what you already earned

Quote to order, order to invoice, invoice to the ledger, ledger to the filing. Every handoff is a place where money quietly disappears. Put each leak on a ledger in dollars, then close it lever by lever.

PRINCIPLE 4

Every number needs a source

Sellers and engineers trust a price or a refusal when they can see why: the rule, the code section, the catalog page. Black-box answers get overridden, and overrides are where the leaks start.

PRINCIPLE 5

The customer owns the logic

Pricing and product rules are a company's crown jewels. They should run in the customer's environment, under the customer's control, governed and audited. Not locked in a vendor's admin screens.

PRINCIPLE 6

Prove it before the big bet

Enterprise programs taught me how much a long, unproven rollout costs. Nteli works in fixed-fee phases: the first one runs alongside your current process, so you see the result before you commit.

06 · The Leak Ledger Method

Find the leaks across your Quote-to-Comply pipeline. Close them lever by lever.

The four questions never change. How the money moves does, so the Leak Ledger Method follows three motions, and each industry gets its own levers.

DIRECT · MANUFACTURERS

Quote → Order → Recognize → Comply

Configure-to-order products sold direct. The leak starts in the quote and follows the order into the books.

CHANNEL · SEMICONDUCTOR, PHARMA

Agree → Transact → Settle → Comply

Price lives in contracts and design registrations; the leak flows out through Ship & Debit claims and chargebacks.

RECURRING · SAAS

Quote → Contract → Recur → Comply

Termed contracts that keep transacting; the leak hides in renewals, uplifts and ratable revenue.
