Guide · AI and quoting

Can ChatGPT or Claude quote configured industrial products?

The short answer: not on their own. A chat assistant doesn't know your price book, your options or the codes your product must meet, and it will still give you a confident number. Connected to a quoting engine, yes: the assistant reads the RFQ and asks what's missing, the engine sizes, checks and prices from your rules, and the assistant shows the result with its sources.

What goes wrong when an assistant quotes alone

Paste a price sheet into a chat and ask for a quote, and you'll get something that reads well. The trouble is underneath. A language model predicts text; it doesn't calculate from rules. Sooner or later it will:

  • Describe a configuration that doesn't exist, because the combination sounded plausible.
  • Apply a discount nobody approved, or round a price that shouldn't move.
  • Size a component wrong: a breaker, a conductor gauge, a cooling unit, with no code table behind it.
  • Give a different answer the second time you ask the same question.

The assistant is good at the conversation. It shouldn't own the number.

How it works with a quoting engine

ChatGPT apps and Claude connectors can call outside tools through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). A quoting engine exposed this way splits the job: the assistant handles the conversation, the engine owns every check and every price.

RFQ, spec or email → Assistant pulls out the facts and asks what's missing → Engine sizes and checks against your rules → Engine prices from your price book → Quote card with sources → Assistant explains it

The assistant never invents a number: if a request can't be built, the engine refuses it and gives the reason, and the assistant passes that on.

What to test before you trust one

  • Same facts, same price. Ask twice. A rule engine gives the identical quote; a bare model often doesn't.
  • Every number has a source. The code table, the rating, the catalog page or the price book line.
  • Bad requests get refused. Ask for something that can't be built and see whether it says no, and why.
  • Your prices, not the internet's. The quote should come from your price book, not from list prices the model found online.
  • Your data stays yours. Rules and quote history should live in your own workspace.
