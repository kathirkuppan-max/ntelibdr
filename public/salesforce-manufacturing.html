Manufacturing Cloud or Revenue Cloud?

NeedManufacturing CloudRevenue Cloud
Run-rate businessSales agreements with planned volumes and prices over timeNot its focus
ForecastingAccount-based forecasts from agreements, orders and opportunitiesNot its focus
RebatesRebate programs, tiers and payoutsNot its focus
Configured productsNot its focusProduct catalog, configurator rules and guided selling
Pricing and quotingAgreement pricingPricing procedures, discounts, approvals and quote documents
Orders and billingOrders against agreementsOrders, fulfillment orchestration and billing

Many manufacturers need both: agreements and forecasts for the repeat business, and Revenue Cloud for the configured and engineered quotes. Both run on the core Salesforce platform, so accounts, products and orders are shared.

Where manufacturers lose money between quote and order

  • Quotes that can't be built. Options left off or combined wrongly, caught by engineering after the customer has a price.
  • Discounts beyond policy. Deals priced deeper than peer deals, with no record of why.
  • Orders retyped into the ERP. A different price, quantity or ship-to than the accepted quote.
  • Channel pricing paid wrong. Dealer discounts and rebates that don't match the agreement.

Each is fixable in the Salesforce design, if you know where it's happening. We start by finding out, deal by deal, with a Leak Ledger. More on the four levers in Quote-to-Order for manufacturers.

FAQ

What does a Salesforce partner for manufacturing do?

Sets up Salesforce around how a manufacturer sells: Manufacturing Cloud for sales agreements, account-based forecasting and rebates; Revenue Cloud or CPQ for configuring, pricing and quoting products; Experience Cloud portals for dealers and distributors; and the integration that turns accepted quotes into ERP orders.

What's the difference between Manufacturing Cloud and Revenue Cloud?

Manufacturing Cloud manages the run-rate business: sales agreements with planned volumes and prices, account-based forecasting and rebate programs. Revenue Cloud handles the transaction: configuring products, pricing, quoting, ordering and billing. Manufacturers that sell both run-rate and configured or engineered products often use both.

Does Salesforce replace our ERP?

No. Your ERP stays the system of record for items, inventory, production and accounting. Salesforce handles accounts, opportunities, agreements and quotes, and sends accepted quotes to the ERP as orders. We've connected Salesforce to SAP, NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics, Epicor and Infor.

Can our dealers and distributors quote in Salesforce?

Yes. An Experience Cloud partner portal lets dealers register deals, configure and quote within your rules, and place orders, while you see the pipeline and pricing in one place.

We're on Salesforce CPQ. Should we move to Revenue Cloud?

CPQ is end of sale, not end of life, so there's no forced date. Move when you have a business reason, and plan either way. Our guide covers when and how to migrate.
