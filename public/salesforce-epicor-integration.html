Salesforce Epicor integration for manufacturers

By Kathiravan Kuppan, Salesforce Premier Partner · Updated November 16, 2026
The short answer: A Salesforce Epicor integration for a manufacturer turns an accepted Salesforce quote into an Epicor sales order, with the right customer, ship-to, parts and configuration, and brings order status, shipments and invoices back to the rep. Epicor stays the system of record for parts, jobs and accounting. Routes include Epicor's REST APIs and BAQs, middleware such as MuleSoft or Boomi, and packaged AppExchange connectors.

Start with the quote-to-order flow. That's where manufacturers lose the most: orders retyped into the ERP at a different price, the wrong ship-to, or options left off. Everything else can follow.

Ways to connect Salesforce and Epicor

Epicor REST APIs and BAQs

Kinetic exposes REST services, and Business Activity Queries can be published as REST endpoints. Full control over what moves.

Middleware

MuleSoft, Boomi or Jitterbit with Epicor connectivity. Monitoring, retries and room for other systems.

Packaged connectors

AppExchange sync apps for Epicor, and a Data Cloud (Data 360) connector for Kinetic that brings Epicor data into Salesforce's data platform. Quick to start; check how each handles quotes, configured parts and ship-tos.

We're tool-agnostic. The right choice depends on what your IT team already runs and how many systems will join later. MuleSoft is the common choice when Salesforce is the hub.

Where Salesforce Epicor integrations go wrong

  • Two quote screens. Epicor has its own quote entry. Keep quoting in Salesforce and create the Epicor sales order on acceptance, or estimators and reps end up maintaining two quotes.
  • Ship-tos. One Epicor customer with many ship-tos is common. Model them in Salesforce (child accounts or address records) so the order lands at the right site.
  • Configured parts. If you use Epicor's configurator, the inputs on the order must match what the Salesforce quote configured. Mismatches show up as the wrong build.
  • Price lists. Epicor customer price lists and Salesforce price books will drift unless one is the source.
  • Prophet 21 vs Kinetic. P21 (distribution) and Kinetic (manufacturing) have different data models and APIs. Name the product before scoping.

Quote to order: the flow that pays for the integration

For a manufacturer, the integration isn't about syncing records; it's about the quote the customer accepted becoming the order you build and bill. With Revenue Cloud or CPQ in Salesforce, the accepted quote creates the Epicor order with the same configuration and price, and the order and invoice come back linked to it. That's what lets you check, deal by deal, that what was quoted is what was ordered and billed. We call that check a Leak Ledger.

FAQ

Does Salesforce have a native Epicor integration?

Not in Sales Cloud. Options are Epicor's REST APIs and BAQs, middleware such as MuleSoft or Boomi, AppExchange connectors, or the Data Cloud (Data 360) connector for Epicor Kinetic, which brings Epicor data into Salesforce's data platform rather than creating orders.

Should quotes live in Salesforce or Epicor?

For sales-led quoting, in Salesforce, with the Epicor sales order created on acceptance. If estimating stays in Epicor for engineered jobs, decide which quote is the customer-facing one and link the two.

How do we handle configured parts?

The configuration chosen on the Salesforce quote has to arrive in Epicor as the same configuration. That means mapping configurator inputs explicitly, not just passing a part number.

How long does a Salesforce Epicor integration take?

Core flows (customers, ship-tos, parts, quote to order, status back) can go live in weeks. Configured parts and price list ownership add scope.
