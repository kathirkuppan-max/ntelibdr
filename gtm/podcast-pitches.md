### CPQ Podcast → info@novuscpq.com
Subject: CPQ Podcast guest idea: a complete quote is not a correct one

Hi Frank,

Your recent episodes on Revenue Cloud Advanced and constraint-based configuration are close to
my own work, and I'd like to offer an episode idea.

Working title: "A complete quote is not a correct one: what enterprise configurators teach
mid-market manufacturers."

I spent ten years building product configurators and pricing systems at Cisco, Dell and IBM.
The lesson I keep seeing at mid-market manufacturers is that quoting tools now protect
completeness (every line priced, nothing left off) far better than correctness (whether the
configuration can actually be built, certified and installed as quoted). A control panel quoted
complete at a 35 kA rating that ships into 65 kA of available fault current is the expensive
kind of error, and it surfaces at commissioning, not at quoting.

Topics we could cover:
- why enterprise CPQ assumptions (a dedicated rules team, sellers clicking through a
  configurator) break at 50- to 300-person manufacturers;
- where Salesforce CPQ and Revenue Cloud Advanced fit, and where the estimating desk needs
  something else, from someone who implements both;
- agentic quoting: AI reading the RFQ and running the conversation while a deterministic rules
  engine computes every price and check;
- governing rule changes (code cycles, tariffs, efficiency standards) so a quote desk absorbs
  them instead of leaking.

Today I run Nteli in Austin, a Salesforce Premier Partner that also builds quoting software for
configure-to-order manufacturers. Happy to keep it practical rather than a product pitch.

Kathiravan Kuppan
Founder & CEO, Nteli · https://nteli.io

### Copperberg Conversations → info@copperberg.com
Subject: Podcast guest idea for Copperberg Conversations: where aftermarket margin leaks

Hi Copperberg team,

I'd like to suggest an episode for Copperberg Conversations on Manufacturing Matters, on
pricing and aftermarket.

Working title: "Where aftermarket margin quietly leaks, between the quote and the invoice."

Spare parts and service are where many manufacturers earn their best margin, and also where it
slips away unnoticed. In the reconciliation work we do, the same leaks keep appearing:
- service and spare parts quoted off the price book, so each estimator prices the same part
  differently;
- pass-through costs absorbed because no surcharge rule fires when material prices move;
- invoices billed off the customer's contract price, which customers rarely flag when the error
  favours them;
- rebates and channel claims paid without checking them line by line against the agreement.

The conversation would cover why these leaks never show up on a single report (each system
looks fine on its own; the gap sits between the quote, the order and the invoice), and the
practical checks that catch them before the money moves.

About me: I spent ten years building product configurators and pricing systems at Cisco, Dell
and IBM, and now run Nteli in Austin, Texas, working with manufacturers on quote-to-cash and
pricing.

Kathiravan Kuppan
Founder & CEO, Nteli · https://nteli.io
