A point of view for your manufacturing accounts

Agentforce Manufacturing and Agentforce Revenue Management for specialty manufacturers, with the product rules already in hand.

Specialty manufacturers build to order. Their quotes come from spreadsheets, and one or two people know which combinations can be built. That is why they need Agentforce Revenue Management, and it is also where rollouts stall: nobody has time to write the product rules down. Nteli arrives with them.

What Nteli brings to each account

  • Ready-made product rules, pricing and guided flow for 23 product lines, set up in the Product Configurator and pricing procedures. Lines include control panels, power packages, instrumentation, cable assemblies, HVAC, pumps, water treatment, transformers, lifts, surgical systems, and NRE services for medical device prototyping.
  • Channel Revenue Management for semiconductors: distributor claims checked line by line against Sales Agreements, POS and stock, each with its reason.
  • Checks that stop what can’t be built, before any price, with the reason and the priced ways out.
  • A forecast from what really ships: Sales Agreements, order history and weighted opportunities in the Account Forecast.
  • Agentforce actions on every step. Agentforce proposes; the rep approves.
  • COGS-based pricing: a cost-to-net waterfall with margin guidelines, and approvals only when a person is needed.

What it means for your deals

  • Licenses stay yours. Nteli’s fees are services only: Agentforce Manufacturing, Agentforce Revenue Management, Agentforce and Sales Cloud are all your sale.
  • A smaller first step for the customer. A fixed $25K start sits easily beside the license deal.
  • Less risk in the rollout. The product rules exist on day one, so the customer’s engineers review them instead of writing them.
  • One story your sellers can repeat for any manufacturing prospect. Only the product changes.
  • Direct and through the channel: dealer quoting and distributor claim checking for those that sell both ways.
